# `pattern_stamp` — the account a declarative stamp owes, and the shell one is

Two subjects, one kind. Planning the generation of the machine's
`scope_guard_version!` stamp — which authored pattern was instantiated, with
which typed arguments, on whose declared facts, producing which complete output
set, and what would make that account stale. And rendering the COUPLED-SEAT
stamp, the engine-authored shell the publication road lands in the machine as
visible source so that every collection-shaped refusal family gets its seat
behind a wall the compiler keeps.

## The plan, not the stamp

For the scope-guard subject nothing here writes a byte of the stamp. It is a
`macro_rules!` in the machine's identity home — the home that owns the Class-C
shape stamps it — and this home only states the account: a `ProjectionPlan` over
[`PatternStampProjection`](crate::planning::PatternStampProjection) like any
other. Building one here is what shows the plan family carries a *declarative*
stamp and not only a derive: the pattern kind is in the sealed roster, and this
home shows the roster meant it.

## Anchors in, nothing minted

Every identity a stamp plan carries names something the machine owns — the
captured declaration the invocation is, the closed graph, the profile, the
authored pattern, the instantiation, the typed arguments, the generated unit,
and the byte role the published artifact is read under.
The caller supplies them as [`ScopeGuardStampAnchors`]; this home reads them and
adapts none. Nothing here observes the stamp's expansion, and nothing here
decides what the stamp means.

The captured declaration is the one address the content walked in with, so the
ENTRY ACCOUNT is built from it here and moved into the plan. That account is the
single holder of what the content is and what it stands on: the intent identity,
the watch set, the explanation's causing-declaration answer, and the origin node
are four readings of it, and no second list of content dependencies forms
anywhere on this road.

## What the coupled-seat stamp is for

The machine declares its collection-shaped refusals as a body carrying ONE
private seat — band 00's `AdmittedPrefix`, holding the established issues and the
coverage claim the same construction amounts to — read back through `issues()`
and `posture()`. Rust's privacy is MODULE-scoped, so a body declared beside the
rest of a home's types puts that seat within reach of every other type, function
and implementation in the file, and whether one of them writes it is a whole-file
audit rather than a fact.

The stamp ends that. Its expansion is a module of its own whose ENTIRE content is
the stamp's output — the seat, the two readers, the mint road where one was
declared, and the family declaration — and the caller-coordinate re-export that
publishes the family out of it. A `macro_rules!` expansion is closed: no second
`mod` block and no hand-written item can be added to a module that exists only
inside one. So the complete set of roads to the private seat is the set the stamp
writes, and it is the machine's own compiler that establishes it.

## The two grammars, and the reach

A seat declares which mint road it asks for, and the two are not one form with an
optional clause: [`SeatMint::ReadersOnly`] is the stated posture of a family
whose issues no road in the machine can yet assemble, and
[`SeatMint::EstablishedUnder`] names the admission profile a mint stands under.
Neither is a missing profile.

The reach is transported and never copied. A stamped item sits one module deeper
than the coordinate the caller wrote its visibility at, so the front arm renders
the caller's literal tokens at the caller's coordinate and
[`SeatVisibility::transported`]'s answer one module in. `macro_rules!` cannot
transport an opaque `vis` fragment, so the front door spells every reach it
admits and refuses a forwarded one with the machine's own compile-time refusal —
a guessed reach publishes somebody's private seat, and nothing downstream would
report it.

## The publication road's admission rule

Default generation writes nothing to disk. This artifact needs the publication
road, and the rendering says why in a typed [`InsufficiencyGround`] rather than
leaving it to a reader's judgment: one definition that several files reach
([`InsufficiencyGround::CrossFileArtifact`]), minting the module and macro names
those files then write down
([`InsufficiencyGround::IdentifierMinting`]). Declaration-site generation writes
tokens where the declaration stands and nowhere else, and a core-local stamp
cannot write itself.

The ground rides on [`StampPublicationRecord`], beside the three questions the
machine's own publication receipt asks — which unit, what its staged bytes must
satisfy, and what the unit contains. That record is a STATEMENT and never a
receipt: the machine's evidence home owns the receipt and every value on it, and
this side holds its own answers independently so the publication operation
compares two produced values rather than one value with itself. The manifest is
read off the coverage the artifact was rendered from, so a manifest row and the
seat it is about cannot disagree.

## The seats

`types.rs` declares — the two anchor records, the two limit families, the four
closed rosters, the seat's declared facts, the plan statement, the rendering
refusal, and the published artifact with the record it pairs with. Its own child
`type_guard.rs` holds every road that reaches a private seat: a path's segments,
a seat's two spellings, the exported name, the coverage's two namespaces, and the
one composition road, which is what makes "a published stamp never seats one
family twice" a shape rather than a rule. `type_contract.rs` states the
declarative tables: each limit family's authority and magnitude written together,
the visibility transport, and the road from a declared mint to the form the stamp
definition is written from. `plan.rs` is both planning roads — the account the
scope-guard stamp owes, and the reading that turns a plan into the statement of
what its artifact will be. `render.rs` is the token half: the front grammar, the
internal arms, the seat module they transcribe, and the one invocation each
covered seat is migrated to.

The alphabet every rendered identifier is admitted by is not declared here. It is
the wall carrier's own
([`is_rendered_identifier`](crate::test_descriptor::is_rendered_identifier)),
because a second copy would agree with that one until one of them was edited.
